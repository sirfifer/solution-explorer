# Grant policy

How requests for free or discounted use of Solution Explorer are decided. This
is guidance for the copyright holder, published so that the decisions look
consistent rather than arbitrary. It is not a license and creates no
entitlement. See [COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md](../../COMMERCIAL-LICENSE.md) for the
public process and [ADDITIONAL-PERMISSIONS.md](../../ADDITIONAL-PERMISSIONS.md)
for the register of what has actually been granted.

## The principle

The default under [LICENSE](../../LICENSE) is deliberately narrow so that every
commercial permission is a decision, not an accident. The default in practice
should be generous. A narrow license with a generous grant policy keeps the
choice; a broad license gives the choice away once and forever.

Two failure modes to avoid, in order of cost:

1. **Giving away the paying case.** A company runs it on its own code for free
   and never talks to us. This is the failure the license exists to prevent.
2. **Being stingy with the free case.** A student, a maintainer, or a small
   open source project bounces off a permission wall and tells other people it
   is closed. This costs reputation and adoption, and the revenue protected is
   zero.

## Grant by default

Grant free use, on request, with a light touch:

- Open source projects with no commercial sponsor and no paid tier.
- Independent maintainers, students, researchers, and educators, where the
  license does not already cover them.
- Public benefit corporations and mission-driven organizations, where the use
  is in service of the mission.
- Conference talks, courses, books, blog posts, and other work that describes
  or teaches the tool.
- Journalism and security research.

For these, a scoped grant recorded in `ADDITIONAL-PERMISSIONS.md` is enough. No
negotiation, no fee.

## Grant, but scope it and time-box it

- **Company evaluation and proof of concept.** Free, non-production, named
  scope, fixed end date, typically 30 to 90 days. Renewable on request. This is
  the normal on-ramp and should never feel like an obstacle.
- **Open source projects with a commercial parent or a paid tier.** Often yes,
  for the open source work only, explicitly excluding the commercial product.
- **Pilot use inside a company** where a paid license is the expected outcome.

## Do not grant free use

- Any for-profit use on the company's own production code, absent a specific
  reason.
- Consultancies and agencies using it in billable client work.
- Embedding in a product or service sold to others. This is an OEM
  conversation, not a grant.
- Anyone building a substitute for Solution Explorer.
- Anyone who has already been using it commercially without a license and is
  asking retroactively to avoid paying rather than to come into compliance.

## Judgment calls to make deliberately

- **A large, well-funded institution that the license already covers for free.**
  A national lab or a major university gets free use automatically under
  PolyForm Noncommercial. That is a known cost of choosing this license, and it
  is accepted. Do not try to claw it back by reinterpreting the license.
- **An open source project owned by a large vendor.** The project may deserve a
  grant on its merits while the vendor does not. Scope the grant to the project
  and name it.
- **A company doing genuinely good work.** Generosity here is legitimate and is
  the reason for keeping the choice. Still scope it, still time-box it, still
  record it.

## Process

1. Request arrives by email or issue.
2. Decide the category above. If it is a grant, write the scope in one sentence
   before anything else. If the scope cannot be stated in one sentence, it is
   not ready to grant.
3. Send the grant in writing, with a term.
4. Record it in `ADDITIONAL-PERMISSIONS.md` and commit that change.
5. Diary the end date.

Every grant must be recorded. An unrecorded grant is indistinguishable from
inconsistent enforcement, and inconsistent enforcement is what makes a
restrictive license expensive to defend later.
