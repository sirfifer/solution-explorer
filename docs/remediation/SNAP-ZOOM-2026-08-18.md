# Double-tap snap zoom: built, measured, and judged

Written 2026-08-18. Task brief: `HANDOFF-SNAP-ZOOM.md`. Background:
`COMPREHENSION-STUDY-2026-08-17.md`.

## Verdict in one paragraph

It works, and it earns its place on a phone only. On a 390x844 phone the iOS
client level fits at 0.304 zoom with 91x46px nodes that no one can read; one
double tap lands at 0.85 zoom with 255x128px nodes, framed on the Tab Bar, and
a second tap returns to the whole level. That is exactly the gap the "heroes
never aggregate" decision left open, and it closes it. On a laptop and a large
display the same gesture is close to a no-op in value: the node-budget work
already keeps those levels at or above the readability floor, so a double tap
buys 25 to 35 percent more size and costs between a quarter and a third of the
visible nodes. Nothing about it is broken there; it is just not solving a
problem. Whether the gesture FEELS right in the hand is not something a
headless browser can answer, and that is the part still owed to physical
testing.

## What was built

A double tap on empty canvas toggles the view between two states.

- **Fit**: the whole level on screen, the same `fitView({padding: 0.15})` the
  layout already uses.
- **Read**: zoomed to a size the labels can be read at, framed on the most
  important component at this level.

Which state the next tap goes to is decided from where the view actually IS,
not from a remembered flag, so pinching, panning, or a re-layout in between
cannot desynchronise the toggle. The split point is the geometric mean of the
two zooms, because zoom is multiplicative: zoomed out means "take me in to
read", zoomed in means "take me back out to the level".

Read zoom is `max(0.85, fitZoom * 1.25)`, capped at the canvas maximum of 2.
The 0.85 is a comfortable reading size (a node renders 238x119 at that zoom)
and sits clear of `READABLE_ZOOM` (0.6), which is the floor the layout treats
as unreadable rather than a target. The 1.25 factor guarantees Read is always a
real move in from Fit, including on a display where the level already fits
readably; without it a double tap on a large screen would zoom OUT or do
nothing, and the toggle would read as broken.

What Read frames is the most important component on the canvas, ranked by
criticality, then number of connections, then file count, then name. That is
the SAME comparator the store uses to decide which components are on the canvas
at all. It used to live inline in `computeDrillLevelView`; it now lives in
`viewer/src/utils/importance.ts` and both sides call it, so the set that is
shown and the node that is framed cannot drift apart.

### Gesture detection

Two `pointerdown` events at the same screen point (5px slop) inside 500ms, the
same way the node drill is detected, not the browser's `dblclick`. The first
tap deselects, which closes the detail panel, which resizes the canvas (1184px
to 864px, measured) and slides content roughly 160px sideways, so the second
press routinely lands on a different element and no `dblclick` ever fires. Only
the FIRST press has to be on the pane; requiring the second one to be on the
pane as well would fail in exactly the case the detection exists for.

`pointerdown` covers mouse and touch on one path. A pinch's second finger is
not the primary pointer and lands far away, so it is rejected twice over. A
press that travels (a pan) drops the candidate on `pointerup`, so returning to
the start point later cannot be mistaken for the second half of a tap pair. The
press that triggers a snap has its click swallowed, so a node that slid under
the second tap does not get selected.

A Read snap taken moments before a re-layout is re-applied after that layout
lands rather than being fitted over. Without it, the panel-close resize that
the first tap causes would re-budget the level and `fitView` straight over the
snap. Verified: with the detail panel open, a double tap on empty canvas closes
the panel, re-layouts, and still holds 0.85 zoom.

### Where the code is

- `viewer/src/utils/snapZoom.ts`: all of the decisions, pure and testable.
- `viewer/src/utils/importance.ts`: the one importance comparator.
- `viewer/src/store.ts`: `computeDrillLevelView` now calls that comparator.
- `viewer/src/components/ArchitectureGraph.tsx`: gesture, snap, re-layout
  survival, shared zoom limits.
- `viewer/src/__tests__/snapZoom.test.ts`: 16 tests over the decisions. The
  gesture plumbing is deliberately not unit-tested; it is verified in a browser.

## Measured on the real dataset

Production build of this branch, served against the mirrored UnaMentis demo
(254 components, split mode, AI-enriched), driven in a real Chrome. The level
is the iOS client drill level in every row. "Canvas" is the graph area the
viewer actually gets after the header and the collapsed summary banner.

| Viewport | Canvas | Fit zoom | Node at fit | Nodes in view | Read zoom | Node at read | Nodes in view |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Phone 390x844 | 390x443 | 0.304 | 91x46 | 7 of 7 | 0.85 | 255x128 | 2 of 7 |
| Laptop 1440x900 | 1184x768 | 0.678 | 217x99 | 13 of 13 | 0.85 | 272x125 | 9 of 13 |
| Large 2560x1440 | 2304x1327 | 0.632 | 202x93 | 41 of 41 | 0.85 | 272x125 | 26 of 41 |

Top level on the phone, the other case the handoff called out: fit 0.235 with
85x53px nodes, read 0.85 with 306x190px nodes framed on the management server.

Read the phone row and the other two rows differently. On the phone, fit is far
below the 0.6 readability floor and the snap is the only way to read anything.
On the laptop and the large display the shrink loop already holds fit above the
floor, so both states are readable and the snap is trading breadth for size.

Every case toggled back to the exact fit zoom on the next double tap.

### Also verified in the browser

- Wheel zoom (the desktop stand-in for pinch) still zooms freely, and a double
  tap from any hand-set zoom snaps to the sensible state rather than fighting it.
- A single tap on empty canvas still only deselects. A pan then a tap does not
  snap.
- Node double-click still drills (8 top-level nodes to 7 at the iOS level).
- Synthetic touch-type pointer events drive the snap correctly.
- No JavaScript console errors. The only network 404s are the expected optional
  `live-config.json` and `publication.json`.

### Not verified

Real touch hardware. The browser tooling available here attaches to a running
Chrome and cannot emulate touch, so the touch path was exercised with
touch-type pointer events rather than real fingers. Two-finger pinch against
the double-tap detector, and whether a double tap on empty canvas is
comfortable to aim on a real phone, are open until physical testing.

## Two things that need your call

Both are packaged below with evidence, options, and a recommendation. Neither
blocks anything; the code is committed and working under the recommended
answer to each.

### Decision 1: Read frames priority, but should it also slide back inside the level?

**Situation.** "Centers on priority" taken literally puts the most important
node dead centre. On the UnaMentis iOS level the most important node (Tab Bar,
critical, 5 connections) is at the top-left corner of the ELK layout, so
centring it fills the rest of the screen with empty canvas. Measured before the
fix, at 2560x1440: 659px of nothing to the left of the content, with real nodes
sitting just off the right edge. 11 of 41 nodes visible.

**Why it matters.** The whole point of Read is to spend the screen on something
worth reading. A third of a large display showing nothing is the opposite.

**What is in the code now (option A).** Centre on priority, then slide back
inside the content bounds the way a map viewer does, with 24px of padding. The
priority node stays fully framed either way; what changes is that the rest of
the screen shows the level instead of nothing. Large display went from 11 to 26
of 41 nodes visible. On the phone it gains one extra readable node.

| Option | What it does | Effort | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Clamp to content (in the code now)** | Centre on priority, then slide back inside the level | done | Large display: 11 to 26 nodes visible. Laptop: 4 to 9. No empty canvas ever | On a phone the priority node lands in the top-left corner rather than centred, and its top edge tucks slightly under the breadcrumb pill |
| B. Pure centring | Priority node dead centre, always | 5 min to revert | Literal reading of your spec. Predictable. Priority node never near an edge | Wastes up to a third of a large display. Fewer nodes readable everywhere |
| C. Clamp with a larger top pad | A, but pad enough to clear the breadcrumb bar | ~20 min | Fixes the one cosmetic nit in A | Another magic number tuned to current chrome; will drift when chrome changes |
| D. Clamp only when the level is much bigger than the screen | A on desktop, B on a phone | ~30 min | Best of both per device | Two behaviours for one gesture, harder to predict, more to test |

**Recommendation: keep A, revisit after you have it in your hands.** The
overlap it causes is cosmetic and the space it recovers is not. C is the
obvious follow-up if the overlap annoys you on a real phone, and it is a
one-line change to the padding constant.

**What I need from you:** keep A, or switch to one of B, C, D.

### Decision 2: What should Read do on a display where the level already fits readably?

**Situation.** The node-budget work already holds the laptop (0.678) and the
large display (0.632) above the 0.6 readability floor. So on those, Read is not
rescuing anything: it makes nodes 25 to 35 percent bigger and drops a quarter
to a third of them off screen. The phone is the only measured case where fit is
genuinely unreadable.

**Why it matters.** A gesture that does something pointless on the two biggest
form factors is a gesture people learn to distrust, and this is the same trust
budget the comprehension study says is already thin.

| Option | What it does | Effort | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Leave it (in the code now)** | Always a real step in, everywhere | done | One rule, one behaviour, nothing to explain. Reads as "look closer", which is a fair thing for a double tap to mean | Does not solve a problem on desktop. Costs nodes for size nobody needed |
| B. Suppress Read when fit is already readable | Double tap does nothing on laptop and large | ~30 min | Never does anything pointless | A gesture that does nothing is indistinguishable from a broken one. Worse than A |
| C. Read becomes "frame the priority component and its direct neighbours" | On a big screen it fills the screen with the relevant subgraph instead of a fixed zoom | ~1 to 2 hrs | Genuinely useful on every size. Matches "the most important thing decides what gets framed" more closely than a fixed zoom does | New behaviour to design and test. Needs a rule for a component with no visible neighbours, and a zoom cap so a lone node does not fill the screen |
| D. Only enable the gesture on touch form factors | Phone and tablet get it, desktop does not | ~20 min | Ships it exactly where it pays | Desktop users who would use it lose it. Form-factor detection is a guess |

**Recommendation: A now, and reconsider C after physical testing.** The honest
reason is that the question is about how the gesture feels, and every option
here is cheap to change once you have used it on a real device. Building C
before that is designing against a guess.

**What I need from you:** confirm A for now, or pick one of B, C, D.

## Two observations, not fixed, not in scope

1. **A sub-case of S5 is still broken, and it predates this work.** On a
   desktop, double-clicking a node whose FIRST click opens the detail panel does
   not drill: the panel opens, the canvas shrinks 1184 to 864, the node slides
   about 160px, and the drill never fires. Double-clicking the same node when
   the panel is already open drills correctly. Verified on a production build of
   this branch WITHOUT the snap-zoom work (built from the current branch head
   with these changes stashed, served separately, same dataset, same viewport):
   it fails there identically, so it is not a regression from this work. The
   likely cause is the 500ms staleness check in the drill detector expiring while
   the detail panel's first render blocks the main thread, but that is a
   hypothesis, not a diagnosis.
2. **The expanded summary banner leaves almost no canvas on a phone.** At
   390x844 with the banner in its default expanded state, the graph area is
   effectively unusable, and even collapsed it takes 332 of 844 pixels, leaving a
   390x443 canvas. That collapsed canvas is what every measurement above uses. It
   is worth deciding separately what the banner should do on a phone, because it
   shapes every number in the table.
